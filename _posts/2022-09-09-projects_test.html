Robot-Assisted Drilling on a Curved Surface

Drilling a hole on a Curved Surface with the desired angle is prone to failure. Trajectory planning of a fully automated robotic system to drill a hole can be impractical for complex shapes in the assembly line. We propose a pHRI system that includes the cobot, a spindle mounted to the end-effector of the cobot, and the holographic device. The cobot has been enhanced with haptic guidance and a 6 DoF adaptive admittance controller. In communication with the cobot, the holographic device provides real-time visual guidance to a human operator. Thanks to the proposed system, one can easily manipulate the drill mounted on the robot with one hand comfortably and open holes on a curved surface. Adaptation of admittance controller parameters provides more transparency (i.e., less resistance) while driving in free space, ensuring stability during drilling with the defined desired angle.
The task can be summarized in the following sentences. The operator brings the drill sufficiently close to the target by roughly aligning. As the drill tip approaches the target, the controller parameters are adapted for decreasing transparency to prevent accidental contact. After certain proximity, the haptic guidance module takes the role and fine-tunes the alignment first; then, it constrains the user's movement to the drilling axis only. Afterward, the operator can push the drill into the workpiece with minimal effort to complete the task. The mixed reality interface can vary in two modes depending on the status of the haptic guidance. HoloLens, as a holographic device, displays the red transparent hemisphere and white transparent arrow to address desired drilling angle and position when the haptic guidance is turned on. In the opposite case, Hololens provides the error of azimuth and polar angle along with correction directions as visual feedback.

Technologies: In this project, we used KUKA iiwa collaborative robot equipped with two F/T sensors utilizing Fast Robot Interface (C++) by KUKA. Microsoft HoloLens 1st generation provides visual guidance. I developed Hololens's Application on Unity using Microsoft's Mixed Reality Toolkit (MRTK) with C#. Visual Fiducial Markers were placed on the curved workpiece to allow us to define the position of the augmented virtual objects. The cartesian coordinate frame of the marker has been tracked with Hololens's RGB camera utilizing Kalman Filter. Surface normals of desired hole positions have been derived using meshes of the curved object, which were generated by 3D scanning with Kinect First Generation using the Skanect App. TCP communication has been established between the robot and Hololens.

Subtask Classification for 1 DoF Robot-Assisted Drilling Task

The fixed-parameter admittance controller has the inherent trade-off between transparency (i.e., less resistant robot for the human operator) and stability. Contact with a stiff environment can jeopardize the stability of the pHRI system, and robot-assisted drilling is exemplary for this case. In this project, we propose a framework that paves the way for adapting admittance controller parameters to deal with the trade-off by utilizing the subtask classes for the pHRI task. Subtask classes consist of the Idle, Driving, and Contact phases and can be detected by a trained Artificial Neural Network (ANN) model. Idle subtask is defined to understand the initiation of the task. During the Driving phase, a human operator approaches the target piece to drill and needs less resistance from the robot's side to decrease human effort. On the contrary, the controller should ensure stability during the Contact phase. Thanks to our proposed model, we can detect the subtasks of the pHRI task and adapt the controller parameters according to the result of the classifier. Experiments have been performed with 12 participants to evaluate the proposed architecture's performance. The classifier achieved 98% accuracy when our controller led to 20% less human effort during the Driving subtask and 25% lower oscillation amplitude in the Contact phase.

Technologies: In this study, we used KUKA iiwa collaborative robot equipped with two F/T sensors utilizing Fast Robot Interface (C++) by KUKA. For training the ANN model, I preferred the Tensorflow framework in Python, and we converted it into C++. Participants were able to monitor real-time drilling depth on the screen of another monitor. I developed a GUI based on Windows Presentation Foundation (WPF) with C# to provide real-time information for participants utilizing TCP communication. We performed the linear time-varying stability analysis to derive time-varying modes for ensuring the stability of our proposed adaptive controller.

The Enhanced App for ATI F/T Sensors

For the physical Human-Robot Interaction (pHRI) systems, measuring forces applied by humans or the environment are prerequisites for engaging to interaction controller. ATI's force/torque transducers are preferred for our pHRI studies. F/T sensors are vulnerable to overloading interactions and have safety limits for performing reliable measurements. A sensor might become nonfunctional when interactions exceed the safety limits several times. Moreover, mounting the sensor into the robot's end effector demands attention not to go over the threaded range. Consequently, monitoring voltages and forces/torques are required, especially during assembly. ATI provides the app for visualizing the measurement of one sensor. However, most of our work consists of two F/T sensors. Therefore, I have developed an application to monitor force torque readings simultaneously. The feature list of the application is listed below:

  • Gauge Voltage Saturation Alarm and Visual Warning
  • Force / Torque Saturation Alarm and Visual Warning
  • Biasing sensor independently
  • Plotting the measurements
  • User-friendly GUI for selecting calibration files and device pin configurations

Technologies: C#, Windows Presentation Foundation (WPF)


Autonomous Manipulation and SLAM with the Hybrid Mobile Manipulator

This study was completed as my Bachelor's Graduation Thesis, and it aimed to demonstrate the soft robotic as a part of the mobile manipulator and execute the autonomous pick&place manipulation. Soft robot parts of the hybrid mobile manipulator consisted of the soft gripper, while ClearPath's Husky UGV was utilized as a mobile platform. Owing to the fact that the Covid-19 pandemic emerged, the actual implementation and experimentations had to move in a simulation environment. Gazebo had been preferred to simulate the ROS-enabled robot's execution of defined tasks. The tasks include:

  • The visual recognition of the marked boxed with ARtags.
  • Autonomous Navigation.
  • Pick&Place manipulation of the objects located on top of the marked boxes.

Technologies: A linear programming optimization algorithm has been selected for determining the link lengths. Siemens NX CAD was used to design the rigid parts of the hybrid mobile manipulator when the FEM analysis was executed on the Abaqus application. Arduino has controlled the actuators and enabled communication with ROS. Thanks to the attached RGB Camera and 2D LIDAR, ROS Navigation Stack consists of gmapping, Adaptive Monte Carlo Localization with Extended Kalman Filter, and OpenCV have powered the autonomous navigation and tag tracking. The Datmo Algorithm has assisted the L shape extraction from the point cloud to detect marked boxes. For the simulation platform, the Gazebo application was preferred. A geometric approach has been chosen to solve the serial manipulator's inverse kinematic instead of the MoveIt! package. You can watch the related videos and access the full text of the thesis below. Note that pick&place manipulation could not be captured since the physics engine of the Gazebo did not support actuation with deformable objects.
