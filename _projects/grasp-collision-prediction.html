--- title: "Sampling-Based Grasp and Collision Prediction for Assisted Teleoperation" pub: grasp-collision theme: shared-autonomy topic: Assisted teleoperation order: 2 summary: >- Neural networks predict constraint costs (collisions, valid pre-grasps) for about a thousand sampled robot configurations in parallel; the system executes the feasible one closest to the operator's VR target, in real time on a bimanual robot. ai_summary: >-

In real-time teleoperation, the operator should decide the rough motion while the robot handles fine adjustments, for example when the operator's view is occluded. The paper frames assisted teleoperation as tracking the operator's target pose as closely as possible subject to constraints (static, self, dynamic and mutual collisions, and valid pre-grasp poses) that a simple state machine switches on and off for task-specific assistance.

Solving this constrained optimization at every step is too slow, so the system samples robot configurations around the current state, predicts every active constraint cost with small neural networks for all samples in parallel, and executes the feasible configuration closest to the operator's target. On two Franka arms controlled from VR, all 12 assisted table-clearing trials succeeded, against 10 of 12 without assistance.
