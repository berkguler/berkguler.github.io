Overview

Many rope tasks, such as routing or untangling, require grasping interior points of a rope that lies freely on a table, with two arms that can each choose where to grasp and how far to move. This creates a large action space, so for model-predictive control the speed of the dynamics model matters as much as its accuracy: a slightly more accurate but much slower model can evaluate far fewer candidate actions per control cycle.

ForwardDLO is a recurrent latent dynamics model with a single shared per-segment decoder. Spatial coupling along the rope is carried by the shared recurrent belief instead of explicit segment-to-segment message passing, and multi-step rollouts are re-grounded on the predicted rope state at every step. Trained on 2.4M simulated transitions, it is evaluated in open-loop prediction, closed-loop shape matching on a bimanual ALOHA robot, and a time-budgeted routing task.

Key results

  • ≈9kcandidate actions per planning call at 30 Hz, 8–22× the closest learned baselines
  • 13%lower long-horizon error on real ropes than the strongest learned baseline
  • 80%success on real-robot S/U/J shape matching (60 runs)

The higher throughput translates into much better success when every model must plan within the same 30 Hz budget, for example when routing a rope through a fence of obstacles in simulation.
