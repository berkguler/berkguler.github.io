--- title: "Learning Sim-Grounded Policies for Bimanual Rope Manipulation" pub: wigginghaus-ws theme: dlo topic: DLO · State representation for learning order: 4 summary: >- Which DLO state representation should a learned policy see? One multi-view snapshot is lifted into a particle rope simulated with XPBD; a policy on this particle state predicts better untangling actions than one on camera images, about 7× faster. ai_summary: >-

Visual imitation-learning policies for rope manipulation suffer when the robot's own grippers hide the rope. This work asks whether the bottleneck is the state representation rather than the policy architecture or the amount of data. From 96 human teleoperation demonstrations of bimanual overhand-knot untangling, two ACT policies are trained on identical data: one on wrist-camera images, and one on a 100-particle rope state that is extracted once from a multi-view RGB-D snapshot and then evolved in an XPBD simulation, so it stays complete even when the rope is occluded.

On a held-out, stiffer rope, the particle-state policy predicts the first grasp-and-pull action with 30.8% lower error, runs 7.2× faster, and uses 46% less GPU memory.
