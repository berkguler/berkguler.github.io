Overview

Visual imitation-learning policies for rope manipulation suffer when the robot's own grippers hide the rope. This work asks whether the bottleneck is the observation space rather than the policy architecture or the amount of data. From 96 human teleoperation demonstrations of bimanual overhand-knot untangling, two Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) policies are trained on identical data: one on wrist-camera images, and one on a 100-particle rope state. The particle state is extracted once from a multi-view RGB-D snapshot and then evolved in an XPBD simulation as the recorded actions are replayed, so it stays complete even when the rope is occluded.

Key results

  • 30.8%lower action error on a held-out, stiffer rope
  • 7.2×faster inference per step than the vision policy
  • 46%less peak GPU memory
