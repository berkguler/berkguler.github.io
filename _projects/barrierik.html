When a teleoperation system blends the operator's command with autonomous assistance, the blended command can collide with obstacles even if both inputs were individually safe. Most prior approaches only discourage collisions through potentials or cost terms, i.e. as a soft constraint. BarrierIK embeds control barrier functions (CBFs) directly into the inverse kinematics (IK) solver, so the post-blend command is safe by construction while the solver still tracks the target pose, respects joint limits, and stays smooth.

In cluttered simulation scenes, filtering blended commands through CBFs at the IK layer reduces safety-violation time and increases minimum clearance without hurting task performance. In a VR teleoperation user study, participants reported higher perceived safety and trust, less interference, and preferred assistance with the safety filter.

My contributions

  • Formulated and implemented BarrierIK, a CBF-constrained IK solver that enforces post-blend safety as a hard inequality constraint alongside standard IK objectives.
  • Designed and ran the simulation benchmarks in cluttered environments, measuring safety-violation time and minimum clearance against baseline blending.
  • Designed and conducted the VR teleoperation user study comparing pure teleoperation with CBF-filtered assistance.
  • Analyzed the objective (clearance, violation time, task performance) and subjective (trust, perceived safety, interference) results.
  • Released the ROS implementation and the Unity simulation and VR teleoperation framework.
