--- title: "Robot-Assisted Drilling on Curved Surfaces with Haptic Guidance" pub: drilling-iros theme: phri topic: pHRI · Haptic guidance & AR order: 1 summary: >- A 6-DoF adaptive admittance controller on a KUKA LBR iiwa, combined with haptic guidance and a HoloLens AR interface, lets an operator drill holes into curved workpieces at a chosen angle with one hand. ai_summary: >-

Drilling into a curved surface at a precise angle is hard by hand, and full automation is often impractical because parts vary and drill points are not always reachable by a pre-planned path. Here the operator moves a cobot-mounted drill with one hand. The admittance damping adapts to the task phase (light while moving freely, stiffer near the target and during drilling), haptic guidance aligns the drill and then constrains motion to the drilling axis, and a HoloLens AR interface shows targets and alignment cues.

With haptic guidance, three naive participants finished faster, with less effort and lower forearm muscle activation; three experienced industrial workers rated the system easy to use, safe, and helpful.

Key results

  • 26%shorter task completion time with haptic guidance
  • 16%less human effort
  • 27%lower forearm muscle activation (sEMG)

My contributions

  • Developed an admittance controller in C++ using the Fast Robot Interface (FRI) of the KUKA iiwa cobot.
  • Developed the mixed-reality interface in C# for Microsoft HoloLens with Unity and the Mixed Reality Toolkit (MRTK).
  • Implemented a Kalman filter to track the 6-DoF motion of the HoloLens and the workpiece from AprilTag fiducial markers.
  • Built a 3D mesh model of the workpiece with a depth camera (Microsoft Kinect).
  • Set up TCP communication between the robot and the HoloLens.
