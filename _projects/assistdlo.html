Ropes and cables are hard to teleoperate: their shape is high-dimensional, and the operator has to judge depth from a 2D video stream. AssistDLO keeps the human in charge of task-level decisions and helps with perception and precise grasping:

  • Rope state estimation. Two wrist-mounted RGB-D cameras trace the rope (SAM 2 segmentation and a Voronoi-based centre line), fused into a 3D point sequence in real time.
  • Visual assistance (VA). The estimated rope is shown as a registered 3D overlay in the operator's VR headset, adding stereo depth cues.
  • Action assistance (SA-CBF). A control barrier function defines a height-field barrier above the table with approach funnels around rope points, so the gripper can descend only through these funnels and grasp precisely without sweeping through other parts of the rope.

In a 22-participant knot-untangling study, SA-CBF helped novices most (71% → 88% success) and worked best on stiffer ropes, while experts and long, compliant ropes benefited more from visual assistance, so the right assistance depends on the user and the material.

Try it: untangle the knot

Untie a loose overhand knot by grasping and moving strands, as the participants did with a bimanual robot. Click a strand to grasp it, drag to move it and release to put it down; you win when the rope no longer crosses itself. Switch on SA-CBF to get the grasp assistance from the paper. Stuck? The knot comes undone when a free end gets back out from under the strand that holds it, and the Hint button shows where.

My contributions

  • Designed the AssistDLO framework, combining real-time multi-view DLO state estimation with a geometry-aware assistance controller.
  • Formulated the SA-CBF controller as a geometry-aware safety funnel around the estimated rope for precise, collision-aware grasping.
  • Designed and ran the bimanual knot-untangling user study (N = 22) across ropes of varying length and rigidity, comparing visual and action assistance against unassisted teleoperation.
  • Analyzed how the effectiveness of assistance depends on operator expertise and rope properties.
