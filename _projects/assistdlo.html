Overview

Ropes and cables are hard to manipulate: their configuration space is effectively infinite-dimensional, their dynamics are nonlinear, and in teleoperation the operator also has to judge depth from a 2D video stream. AssistDLO is an assistive teleoperation framework for deformable linear objects (DLOs) that keeps the human in charge of task-level decisions and helps with perception and precise grasping.

  • Rope state estimation. Two wrist-mounted RGB-D cameras trace the rope (SAM 2 segmentation and a Voronoi-based centre line), and the traces are fused into a 3D point sequence in real time.
  • Visual assistance (VA). The estimated rope is rendered as a registered 3D overlay in the operator's VR headset, adding stereo depth cues and motion parallax.
  • Action assistance (SA-CBF). A control barrier function defines a height-field barrier above the table with Gaussian approach funnels around rope points. A quadratic program keeps the robot close to the operator's command while it descends only through these funnels, so it can grasp precisely without sweeping through other parts of the rope.

Key results

  • 22participants, 352 knot-untangling trials across 4 ropes
  • 71% → 88%task success for novice operators with SA-CBF
  • 17–21%shorter completion time on successful trials with any assistance mode

SA-CBF acts as a skill equalizer: it gives the largest gains for novice users and is most effective on stiffer ropes, while experts and long, highly compliant ropes benefit more from visual assistance. Effective DLO teleoperation therefore needs assistance that adapts to the user and the material rather than a single fixed strategy.

My contributions

  • Designed the AssistDLO framework, combining real-time multi-view DLO state estimation with a geometry-aware shared-autonomy controller.
  • Formulated the SA-CBF controller as a geometry-aware safety funnel around the estimated rope for precise, collision-aware grasping.
  • Designed and ran the bimanual knot-untangling user study (N = 22) across ropes of varying length and rigidity, comparing visual assistance and shared autonomy against unassisted teleoperation.
  • Analyzed how the effectiveness of assistance depends on operator expertise and rope properties.
