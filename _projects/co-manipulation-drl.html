Using the human's dexterity and intelligence and the robot's precision, we can drive and park heavy objects where the human operator wants without the programming in advance. This study's initial aim is to increase efficiency by understanding human motion intention. However, the human force's response is observed by delay due to the object's weight. When a human and a robot carry a heavy object together, a fixed force amplification is a compromise: amplifying the operator's force helps while driving the object, but makes precise parking hard. In this thesis, a neural network reads the operator's recent force and velocity to infer whether they are trying to speed up or slow down, and continuously retunes how strongly the robot amplifies their force. A deep reinforcement learning agent sits on top and adjusts how aggressively that retuning happens, several times a second, trading motion smoothness against physical effort.

Note that the agent does not pick the gain itself; it sets how fast the gain moves toward the value implied by the operator's intent.

My contributions

  • Built an experimental setup for human-robot co-manipulation of a heavy object with a UR5 robot.
  • Developed a deep-learning model in Python with TensorFlow to estimate the human's intention to accelerate or decelerate the object.
  • Developed a deep reinforcement learning agent, working with the intention model, that optimizes task performance with respect to minimum jerk and human effort.
  • Designed the pHRI experiments, collected the data, and performed the statistical analysis.
