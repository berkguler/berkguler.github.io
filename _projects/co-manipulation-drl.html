Overview

Carrying heavy objects is a natural task for humans and robots to share: the human contributes dexterity and judgment, the robot strength and precision, and together they can drive and park a heavy object where the operator wants without programming the motion in advance. Because of the object's weight, the operator's force response lags, so estimating the operator's motion intention helps decide when to amplify or attenuate the human force. A fixed scaling is not efficient across the phases of the task: amplification helps while driving, attenuation while parking.

A deep-learning model provides an adaptive force-scaling factor from the estimated human intention, but it does not guarantee minimal effort or maximal comfort. A model-free optimal control method, deep reinforcement learning, adjusts this scaling factor to reduce human effort and increase comfort.

My contributions

  • Built an experimental setup for human-robot co-manipulation of a heavy object.
  • Developed a deep-learning model in Python with TensorFlow to estimate the human's intention to accelerate or decelerate the object.
  • Developed a deep reinforcement learning agent, working with the intention model, that optimizes task performance with respect to minimum jerk and human effort.
  • Designed the pHRI experiments, collected the data, and performed the statistical analysis.
